# PREDICTION-MARKET ALPHA SYSTEM

Your job is to build a rigorous, fully reproducible prediction-market research and trading system whose purpose is to discover whether genuine, scalable, after-cost trading edge exists.

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

An AI model is NOT considered an edge.

Grok, Jev, LLMs, sentiment agents, news agents and other AI systems are only potential input components.

The system must determine whether they provide incremental predictive value.

The fundamental trading problem is:

`external information`
→ `estimate true probability`
→ `compare against market-implied probability`
→ `calculate uncertainty`
→ `subtract trading costs`
→ `trade only if expected value remains positive`

Formally:

`raw_edge = model_probability - executable_market_probability`

but a trade must require:

`net_edge = raw_edge - costs - uncertainty_buffer`

and:

`net_edge > minimum_required_edge`

Do not optimize solely for historical P&L.

# 4. PROJECT PHILOSOPHY

This project follows the same research discipline as a professional quantitative trading system.

Every strategy must progress through:

`idea`
→ `formal hypothesis`
→ `data feasibility`
→ `pre-registered rules`
→ `historical backtest`
→ `validation`
→ `untouched out-of-sample`
→ `walk-forward`
→ `Monte Carlo`
→ `live shadow mode`
→ `tiny-capital live test`
→ `scale or retire`

A strategy that fails must be retired.

Do not manipulate rules until the backtest becomes profitable.

Do not cherry-pick favorable periods.

Do not fabricate unavailable data.

Do not silently substitute proxy data.

# 5. INITIAL STRATEGY FAMILIES

Build the framework so each strategy is an independent module.

Initially research these four branches.

## PM-W1 — Weather / objective-data mispricing

Research prediction markets whose outcome can be modeled from objective meteorological information.

Potential sources:

- NOAA
- NWS
- weather observations
- historical weather
- ensemble forecasts where freely available
- storm/hurricane data
- precipitation forecasts
- temperature forecasts

Hypothesis:

prediction-market probabilities may sometimes differ materially from probabilities derived from objective weather data.

Important:

Forecast data must be timestamped.

Never use revised forecasts that became available after the hypothetical trade.

Research:

- forecast calibration
- forecast errors by horizon
- station-specific error
- market reaction delay
- spread/liquidity
- resolution source compatibility

---

## PM-C1 — Probability calibration / favorite-longshot effects

Study whether prediction-market quoted probabilities are systematically miscalibrated.

For historical contracts, analyze buckets such as:

0–5%
5–10%
10–15%
...
90–95%
95–100%

Compare:

`market implied probability`

against:

`actual empirical resolution rate`

Test:

- calibration curves
- isotonic calibration
- Platt/logistic calibration
- Bayesian calibration
- logit transforms
- extreme probability behavior
- contract category
- liquidity
- time to resolution

Do NOT blindly copy coefficients from viral bots or GitHub repositories.

Estimate all parameters independently.

---

## PM-S1 — Structured-event models

Find prediction markets where objective structured external data can generate an independent probability estimate.

Candidate categories may include:

- macroeconomic releases
- interest-rate decisions
- inflation
- scheduled economic data
- crypto price thresholds
- sports where legal/platform appropriate
- other objectively resolved quantitative events

Avoid vague events where outcome interpretation is difficult.

Priority goes to markets where:

- resolution criteria are explicit;
- historical data exists;
- external predictors are timestamped;
- many historical observations are available.

---

## PM-M1 — Market microstructure / stale prices

Investigate whether prediction contracts react slowly to information observable in faster underlying markets.

Examples:

- crypto spot/perpetual prices versus short-horizon prediction contracts
- related prediction contracts with inconsistent pricing
- mutually exclusive contracts
- logically constrained contract sets

Measure:

- reaction lag
- spread
- depth
- fill probability
- slippage
- adverse selection
- API latency

Do NOT assume theoretical arbitrage is executable.

Profitability must use executable bid/ask prices.

# 12. RISK POLICY

The viral bots used aggressive Kelly sizing.

Do NOT copy this.

Initial research risk policy:

Normal maximum loss per independent position:

`0.25% of bankroll`

Absolute hard ceiling:

`0.50% of bankroll`

Maximum correlated event-cluster worst-case exposure:

`1.00% of bankroll`

These are starting research constraints, not optimized values.

Use fractional Kelly only if desired sizing is LOWER than hard caps.

Actual position risk:

`min(fractional_kelly_size, strategy_cap, portfolio_cap, liquidity_cap)`

Never:

- martingale
- grid
- double after loss
- chase losses
- remove stop/risk limits because AI requests it
- allow an agent to edit its own hard risk parameters

Risk limits must be deterministic code.

AI never has final authority over risk.

# 13. BANKROLL SURVIVAL

Because the intended starting bankroll may be small, survival is more important than maximizing theoretical growth.

Measure:

- probability of 10% drawdown
- probability of 20% drawdown
- probability of 30% drawdown
- expected maximum drawdown
- longest losing streak
- 95% expected shortfall
- risk of ruin
- recovery time
- volatility of returns

Use at least:

`10,000 Monte Carlo paths`

for promoted strategies.

Track every strategy version.

`PM-W1-v0.1`
